01

The fundamentals

Understand the email architecture

A custom email address such as support@getkit.in is an address under your domain. It does not automatically include an inbox, storage, or the ability to send mail. DNS tells other systems where to deliver mail and which services are authorized to send it.

Incoming email
Sender sends to support@yourdomain.com
Domain MX records identify the receiving mail service
Mail provider receives, forwards, or stores the message
Outgoing email
You compose a message using a mail client
An SMTP provider sends the message
Recipient checks SPF, DKIM, DMARC, reputation, and content

DNS

Publishes the records that direct web and mail traffic.

Mail service

Receives, forwards, stores, or sends messages.

Authentication

Helps receiving systems verify the sender's domain.

Key distinction: Cloudflare Email Routing can forward incoming messages to an existing inbox. It is not, by itself, a conventional hosted mailbox with inbox storage and a general-purpose SMTP submission service. Read Cloudflare's routing documentation.
02

DNS configuration

DNS record architecture matrix

The exact values depend on your hosting and email providers. Use the table as a reference for what each record does—not as a list to paste blindly into a live DNS zone.

Type Name Purpose / example Proxy
A / AAAA @ Points the root domain to the IPv4/IPv6 address supplied by your web host. Provider-dependent
CNAME www Aliases a hostname to the canonical hostname supplied by your host. Provider-dependent
MX @ Identifies the mail servers that receive email for your domain. Use your chosen provider's targets and priorities. DNS only
TXT @ May contain your root-domain SPF policy, depending on the mail services you actually use. DNS only
TXT selector._domainkey Publishes a DKIM public key supplied by the email sender. The selector is provider-specific. DNS only
TXT _dmarc Defines your DMARC policy and optionally the address for aggregate reports. DNS only
Avoid placeholder IPs

Do not use 192.0.2.1 as your real website origin. This address belongs to a documentation range. Obtain the correct target from your hosting provider.

Avoid duplicate SPF records

A domain should have one SPF policy at a given hostname. If multiple services send mail, merge their verified mechanisms into a single policy instead of publishing multiple independent SPF TXT records.

Which DNS service should be authoritative?

  1. 1.Check which nameservers are active at your domain registrar.
  2. 2.Make DNS changes at the provider that hosts your authoritative DNS zone.
  3. 3.If you use Cloudflare nameservers, manage the applicable records in the Cloudflare dashboard.
  4. 4.Keep web records and mail records consistent with the services you have enabled.

04

Sender identity

SPF, DKIM & DMARC explained

These protocols work together, but they solve different problems. Publishing records is only one part of the setup: the sending service must also sign or transmit messages correctly, and the authenticated domain must align with the visible From domain for DMARC to pass.

SPF — Sender Policy Framework

SPF specifies which sending systems are authorized for a domain used in the SMTP envelope identity. It does not directly authenticate the visible From address by itself.

Illustrative syntax

v=spf1 include:PROVIDER-SUPPLIED-DOMAIN ~all

Replace the placeholder with a real mechanism provided by your email service. Never publish the literal placeholder.

~all · SoftFail

Signals that other senders are not authorized, without explicitly requesting a hard fail from SPF alone.

-all · Fail

Marks other senders as unauthorized. Use only after validating the full sending setup.

Lookup limit: SPF permits at most 10 DNS-querying mechanisms during evaluation. Nested includes count too. Exceeding the limit can cause SPF evaluation errors.

DKIM — DomainKeys Identified Mail

DKIM adds a cryptographic signature to outgoing messages. The recipient retrieves the corresponding public key from DNS using the signature's selector and verifies the signature. Your email provider generates the private/public key pair and gives you the DNS record to publish.

Typical record structure

Name: selector._domainkey Value: v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=PROVIDER_GENERATED_PUBLIC_KEY

This is a schematic example, not a usable key. Do not invent a selector or publish a fake public key. Different providers may use different selectors and record formats.

DMARC — Domain-based Message Authentication

DMARC evaluates whether SPF or DKIM passes with alignment to the domain in the visible From header. It then publishes a policy for handling messages that fail DMARC and can direct aggregate reports to a reporting address.

Stage 1

p=none

Monitor authentication results without requesting quarantine or rejection.

Stage 2

p=quarantine

Ask receiving systems to treat failing messages as suspicious.

Stage 3

p=reject

Ask receiving systems to reject messages that fail DMARC.

Move from monitoring to enforcement only after reviewing reports and confirming that legitimate services—including website forms, payment receipts, newsletters, and support systems—are correctly authenticated.

Alignment matters: passing SPF for an unrelated provider domain or passing DKIM with an unrelated signing domain may not satisfy DMARC for your visible From domain. Test the actual messages you send.
05

Incoming messages

Set up Cloudflare Email Routing

Email Routing is useful when you want addresses such as support@getkit.in or contact@getkit.in to forward to an existing inbox. You do not get a separate mailbox merely by creating a routing rule.

01

Confirm your DNS is on Cloudflare

Your domain must use Cloudflare DNS for Cloudflare Email Routing. Verify the active nameservers and select the correct domain in your account.

02

Enable Email Routing

Open the Cloudflare dashboard and navigate to Compute → Email Service → Email Routing. Onboard the domain and review the DNS records Cloudflare proposes to add.

03

Add and verify a destination address

Add an existing inbox, such as a Gmail address, as a destination. Open the verification email and complete verification before creating routing rules.

04

Create your custom address

Add a rule for support, choose the verified destination, and save it. Repeat for other addresses if needed.

05

Test from an independent account

Send a message from a different email account to the new address. Check the destination inbox and spam folder. Confirm that the message arrives before relying on the address for customer support.

MX conflict warning: Email Routing manages the incoming mail records it needs. If you already use Google Workspace or another hosted email provider for incoming mail, do not switch to Cloudflare's MX records without planning the migration. A domain generally cannot use conflicting inbound routing configurations as if they were one mailbox service.

06

Outgoing messages

Sending from your custom address

Receiving and forwarding mail does not automatically give you a way to send from the same custom address. Choose an outbound method separately.

Option A · Personal Gmail

Gmail may allow you to configure a custom From address through Settings → Accounts and Import → Send mail as. The available configuration and sending behavior depend on the address and account.

Important: a forwarding service alone is not an SMTP server. Gmail's interface also does not guarantee that outbound messages will authenticate and align with your domain.

Option B · Dedicated mail provider

Use a provider that supports custom-domain sending and supplies SMTP or another supported sending method, along with domain verification and DKIM instructions. This is generally the more predictable approach for business support mail.

Check whether the service is free, has sending limits, requires a paid plan, or separates inbound mail from outbound delivery.

Do not copy a generic Gmail SMTP recipe blindly

A common example for a Gmail SMTP client is smtp.gmail.com on port 587 with STARTTLS. However, this is not a universal SMTP solution for every custom domain. A personal Gmail account, a Google Workspace account, and a third-party SMTP service have different authentication and sending rules. Use the server, username, authentication method, and port documented for the account you actually use.

SMTP configuration worksheet 
SMTP host:       PROVIDER-SUPPLIED-HOST
Port:            PROVIDER-SUPPLIED-PORT
Encryption:      TLS / STARTTLS as documented
Username:        PROVIDER-SUPPLIED-USERNAME
Password:        Provider-issued credential or app password
From address:    support@yourdomain.com
DKIM:            Enable and publish the provider's DNS record
SPF:             Authorize the actual sending service
DMARC:           Publish a policy after validating alignment

If your provider supports SMTP submission

  1. Confirm the provider supports your custom From domain.
  2. Verify the domain using the provider's requested DNS records.
  3. Enable DKIM and publish the exact record supplied by the provider.
  4. Configure SPF according to the provider's instructions, merging it with existing authorized senders.
  5. Create or review DMARC in monitoring mode before enforcing a policy.
  6. Send test messages to accounts at more than one provider and inspect the authentication results.

Security rule: Never paste your Gmail password, app password, SMTP token, or private DKIM key into this guide, a public repository, a frontend HTML file, or a client-side JavaScript application. Use provider-approved authentication and keep credentials private.

07

Testing & diagnostics

Verify your DNS records

After publishing records, verify that public DNS returns the values you expect. DNS propagation time varies; a record appearing in your dashboard does not by itself prove that every receiver can resolve it or that messages authenticate successfully.

DNS inspection commands

Run in a terminal with dig installed

# Replace example.com with your domain
dig example.com A +short
dig example.com AAAA +short
dig example.com MX +short
dig example.com TXT +short
dig _dmarc.example.com TXT +short

# Replace the selector with the one your provider supplied
dig selector._domainkey.example.com TXT +short

DNS checks

  • • MX targets match your receiving provider.
  • • Only one applicable SPF policy exists per hostname.
  • • DKIM selector and public key match provider instructions.
  • • DMARC is published at _dmarc.yourdomain.

Message checks

  • • Test mail reaches the expected destination.
  • • Outbound mail uses the intended From address.
  • • Inspect SPF, DKIM, and DMARC results in message headers.
  • • Check spam placement and any bounce response.
Tip: In Gmail, open a received test message and inspect its message details or original headers for authentication results. Look for SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail indicators. A DNS lookup alone cannot establish that your outbound mail is correctly aligned.
08

Common failures

Troubleshooting email issues

Email does not arrive at my forwarded inbox
Verify that the destination address has been confirmed, the routing rule is enabled, the domain's MX records point to the intended receiving service, and the message was not filtered into spam. Test from an unrelated email account.
My email goes to spam
Check SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment in actual message headers. Confirm that the service sending the message is authorized, use a consistent From domain, review bounces and message content, and build sending volume gradually. Authentication helps but cannot guarantee inbox placement.
SPF reports PermError or multiple SPF records
Remove duplicate SPF policies at the same hostname and merge legitimate sending services into one record. Review nested includes and keep DNS-querying mechanisms within the SPF lookup limit. Do not delete a service's authorization until you confirm it no longer sends mail for your domain.
Gmail sends from the wrong address or shows “on behalf of”
Recheck the address verification and the exact Gmail Send mail as settings. Some sending configurations may expose the underlying Gmail address. If you require reliable custom-domain authentication, use a supported outbound provider and configure the SPF and DKIM records it supplies.
Email stopped after changing MX records
Compare the current MX records with the active mail provider's required values. Changing MX records changes where new incoming messages are delivered. Restore the correct provider configuration if a change interrupted an existing mailbox service.
DKIM fails even though the TXT record exists
Check the exact selector from the message signature, record name, key value, and provider status. A valid key under a different selector will not verify that message. Some DNS dashboards automatically append the domain to record names, so check for accidental duplication.
